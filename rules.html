⚡ The Awkward Game — Rules

The Premise

Roll, move, land on a tile, face the awkwardness, get judged by your friends. Collect Awkward Points (good) while dodging Shame Points (bad). Highest AP wins; lowest SP breaks ties.

Turn Flow

  1. Roll the die (1–6).
  2. Move that many tiles. Crossing START grants +2 AP.
  3. Resolve the tile — its type decides which deck you draw from.
  4. Respond: do it, bluff it, choose, take the risk, or sabotage.
  5. Get judged: some cards trigger a group vote (Believe/Caught, Forgive/Punish).
  6. Apply the effects, next player rolls.

Tile Types (54 tiles)

  • Scenario (13) — awkward situations; do it for AP, refuse for +1 SP, or bluff.
  • Choice (9) — two awful options, pick one or eat 3 SP.
  • Risk (8) — gamble (coin flip / roll 4+ / group believes you) or chicken out for 1 SP.
  • Sabotage (6) — spend a Sabotage Token 🎟️ on another player, or skip for +1 AP.
  • Group Vote (3) — the table judges you: Forgive or Punish, or splits on a dilemma.
  • Duel (2) — everyone writes an answer, answers are paired, the table votes head-to-head.
  • Safe (7) — nothing happens. Bliss.
  • Chaos (3) — random deck draw, +1 Sabotage Token.
  • Double Trouble (2) — Scenario + Choice back to back, +1 Sabotage Token.
  • Final Combo (1) — Scenario + Choice + Risk; survive all three for +10 AP.

Card Types (202 cards)

  • Scenario (36) — do it (+AP), refuse (+SP), or bluff (group votes Believe/Caught; caught = SP).
  • Choice (36) — option A or B, each with its own AP/SP.
  • Risk (36) — coin / die / believe mechanics, graded into bands (bullseye / good / near / miss).
  • Sabotage (36) — token cost; effects hit a target of your choice.
  • Group Vote (48) — 36 verdicts (majority rules or unanimous mercy) plus 12 dilemmas.
  • Duel (10) — a prompt everyone answers, then anonymous head-to-head votes.

Dilemmas — Split the Room

The table votes YES or NO on a genuinely divisive question. The author earns 1 + round(splitAp × closeness) AP, where closeness is 1 − |yes − no| ÷ voters — so a 50/50 split pays the full 4–6 AP and a lopsided vote pays almost nothing. If 3+ players vote and the table agrees unanimously, the author also takes +1 SP for asking a boring question. The most evenly-split dilemma wins the Most Divisive award.

Duels — Head to Head

Everyone writes one answer (90 characters). Answers are shuffled into anonymous pairs and everybody except the two duelists votes A or B. Payout is vote share: round(4 × votes ÷ voters) AP each, +2 for winning the pairing, +4 on a unanimous sweep, and a tie pays both their share +1. An odd table gives one player a bye (+2 AP); a two-player table has no jury, so both take a flat 2 AP. Winning the most pairings earns the Duel Champion award.

Duplicate answers cancel: if two players write the same thing (ignoring case and punctuation), that pairing skips the vote — the duplicates score 0 and a clean answer wins by default (+2 AP). Safe answers (submitting nothing) earn only half AP and never count as duplicates.

Risk Bands — Bullseye

Risks are graded instead of pass/fail, priced off the card's own reward:

  • Bullseye — die 6, or a unanimous 2+ voter table believes you: double reward.
  • Good — die 5, a believing majority, or coin heads: the reward as written.
  • Near — die 4, or a tied vote: half the reward, no penalty, no shame.
  • Miss — die 3 or less, a doubting table, or coin tails: the penalty +1 SP.

The coin has no middle, so it stays binary. Bullseye from behind: a player who is not leading and nails a bullseye takes an immediate extra roll instead of passing the turn — the cheapest comeback in the game, and the reason late laps get loud.

Screw Token — Answer Now

While another player has a card on the table you may spend your Screw token to rush them: 10 seconds to commit, or the card answers for them (refuse / chicken out / skip). One screw per lap, one on the clock at a time; duels, safe tiles and chaos are immune.

  • The screw lands (the target gains no AP): target −2 AP, +1 SP, attacker +2 AP.
  • It backfires (the target still comes out ahead): attacker −2 AP.

Sabotage with a cost is a decision, not a free action — which makes who you screw a social signal. Rushing the leader is noble; rushing last place is villainous.

Wager to Play — Double or Nothing

Before answering a Scenario, Choice, Risk, Sabotage or Group Vote card you may wager 2 AP on yourself — once per lap, one wager at a time.

  • Come out ahead on AP: your card winnings are doubled and the stake stays with you.
  • Come out even or behind: the 2 AP stake is gone, on top of the card's own punishment.
  • Duels are off the table — they pay the whole table by vote share, so there is no single outcome to bet on.

Trailing players are expected to gamble; the card banner shows a live wager while it rides, and bots wager more when they are behind.

Lap Escalation

AP earned from cards scales with your lap: ×1, then ×2, then ×3 (capped). Penalties, sabotage damage, lap bonuses and the Final Combo bonus are never scaled. Late laps swing harder, so a slow start is never a lost game — and the log shows the multiplier on every payout.

AI Personalities

Chill, Chaotic, Brutal, Honest and Liar each have different odds for bluffing, risk-taking, sabotage and punishment. Brutal hunts the leader. Honest never bluffs (barring chaos rolls). The Liar gets caught constantly and it's wonderful.

Multiplayer

One player creates a room and shares the 6-character code. Everyone joins from their own device. The server holds the deck order and validates every move; clients only receive what they're allowed to know.

House Rules

Anyone may call the safe word and skip a card for +1 SP, no questions asked. The game rewards playing along with the bit — never actual distress.
